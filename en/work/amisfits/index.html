amisfits.fi: one site, two audiences, three languages

Sivistysala ry’s Amisfits campaign tells two audiences about vocational education: young people finishing comprehensive school, and adults.

HueSky built the campaign a single-page site in three languages, the Amisfits test and the student interview videos. The visual identity was designed by Sampsa Voutilainen. Sivista’s own staff update the texts, images and videos themselves.

Starting point

Amisfits is a campaign by Sivistysala ry’s ETEKA project. Its message rests on three promises: amis (Finnish slang for vocational school) gives you direction, activates your dreams and opens doors.

The same message had to reach two very different readers. A ninth-grader and an adult changing careers ask different questions, and both need the answers in Finnish, Swedish or English.

The content is written by Sivista’s own staff, not by the web developer. So the site had to let three content editors update it without breaking the layout or routing every change through the developer.

Solution

The new site is a single page with its own version for each audience and each language. It was hand-built from scratch to Sampsa Voutilainen’s visual identity, without ready-made themes.

One page, two readers

Visitors choose at the top of the page whether they are a comprehensive school student or an adult learner. The structure and the three themes stay the same, but the texts, video and test change with the reader.

If a section works for both as it is, the content editor ticks one box and the youth text is shown to adults too. The same text doesn’t have to be maintained in two places.

Three languages, each with its own address

The site is in Finnish, Swedish and English. Each language version has its own address in that language, for example /fi/nuoret, /sv/unga and /en/youth. Search engines are told which pages are language versions of each other.

A test that collects nothing

The Amisfits test has five questions, and the result appears immediately. Young people and adults have their own questions and feedback.

The test runs entirely in the visitor’s browser. Answers are not sent or stored anywhere, so the test involves no processing of personal data. Printed materials carry a QR code that leads straight to the test.

Students tell it themselves

At the top of the site is a video in which vocational students explain in their own words why they chose amis and what it has given them. The interviews were filmed at the schools using a filming guide written by HueSky: Perho Business College, Business College Helsinki and Hyria.

HueSky edited two videos from interviews with eight students, one for young people and one for adults. Each has a Finnish, Swedish and English version, with the subtitles burned into the picture. Title cards and graphics follow the site’s visual identity.

The same material produced six short vertical videos for social media. The videos are on the campaign’s own YouTube channel, which HueSky set up and handed over to Sivista.

Watch the videos on YouTube: for young people ↗ and for adults ↗ (English versions).

Content stays in the client’s hands

Texts, images, attachments and videos come from the Sanity content management system. Changing a video only takes pasting a YouTube link into a field, and a published change appears on the site in about a minute.

Info boxes and charts are content blocks, not images. That’s why they scale on phones, translate into three languages and can be read by screen readers. Content editors can also hide part of the page without deleting the content.

Sivista’s content editors received training and a written user guide.

Cookies only after consent

Visitor analytics only starts once the visitor accepts statistics cookies. The YouTube player only loads when the visitor starts the video, so the page opens quickly and YouTube sets no cookies before playback. The privacy policy and accessibility statement are on the site in all three languages.

The site went live on 13 November 2026, three days before the agreed target date, so that everything could be tested before the campaign communications began.

  • One piece of content, many versions: audiences and languages come from the same content management system, so a fix is made in one place.
  • The client updates it: Sivista’s staff add and edit content in the browser without the developer.
  • A campaign site with no upkeep: no plugins, themes or database that would need updating during or after the campaign.
  • Visible in search and social shares: every page has its own title, description and share image, and the texts are already in the page’s code.

Technology in brief

  • Site: Astro. The pages are built into ready-made files in advance, so nothing runs on a server for the visitor.
  • Content management: Sanity. Sivista’s content editors update texts, images, attachments and videos in the browser.
  • Hosting: Netlify. HTTPS and publishing are automatic, and publishing content in Sanity updates the site by itself.
  • Videos: editing, subtitles and graphics by HueSky, distributed on YouTube.
  • Visual design: Sampsa Voutilainen.
